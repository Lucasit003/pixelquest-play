What is PixelQuest?

PixelQuest is a pixel-art action RPG for children aged 7 to 12. A child explores a kingdom, fights monsters and bosses, and grows a hero. Every power the hero uses is earned by learning: a power opens only when the child has mastered the topics behind it and the hero has reached its level. Gold, gear and experience points never unlock a power on their own.

Play PixelQuestFree, in the browser, nothing to install.

How learning works

  1. Lesson

    A short illustrated lesson with "try it" questions along the way.

  2. Quiz

    5 questions; a score of 80% opens the next step.

  3. Practice

    25 practised questions (10 for a child's very first topic). A question answered right twice is retired and not asked again.

  4. Mastery check

    10 questions covering every skill in the topic. Passing it masters the topic, which can open a power.
  • No timers on learning, no streaks, no energy that runs out, and nothing lost for missing a day.
  • Nothing to buy: no real-money purchases, no loot boxes, nothing random for sale.
  • Wrong answers never take progress away. A failed check can simply be tried again.
  • The grown-ups' report (Settings, then Grown-ups) shows topics mastered by subject and grade with their standards, lessons finished, questions practised and retired, the next lessons, and which learning opened each power. It can be printed. It opens with the parent PIN, so children do not wander in by accident.

What it looks like

Safety and privacy, today

  • No ads.
  • No chat or messaging with other players. There is no online play at all.
  • No accounts and no sign-in yet. Nobody is asked for an email or a birthday. The only names are the ones a child types for a hero or a learner profile, and they stay on the device.
  • Progress is saved on the device, in the browser's own storage. Clearing the browser's site data deletes it, so the Save settings offer an export file and a save code.
  • No analytics and no tracking. The game loads only its own files from its own site: no fonts, scripts or images from anywhere else.
  • Links that leave the game (the art credits) and the grown-ups' report sit behind the parent PIN.
  • The parent PIN is a lock on this device, not an account password. A grown-up chooses it, entered twice, the first time any grown-up page is opened; there is no way to skip it. Forgot it? Tap "Forgot PIN?" on the gate to start a 24-hour wait. While it runs the gate shows when the PIN will clear, and entering the PIN cancels it. After the wait, a sum on screen clears the PIN and a new one is chosen. Saves and learning are never touched.

The game is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub keeps standard server logs of visits (such as IP addresses); see GitHub's privacy statement.

A full privacy policy will come before accounts open.

How to start

  • On a computer: open the game in Chrome or Safari. Keyboard: W A S D to move, J to attack, E to talk and open doors, the number keys for powers.
  • On a phone: hold it sideways. A joystick appears under the left thumb and the attack and power buttons under the right.
  • On iPhone: Safari can't hide its bars for a website. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and open PixelQuest from its new icon. It fills the whole screen, like an app.

For teachers: a class can share devices. Each child picks their own name under "Who is playing?" on the title screen, and each learner's progress is kept separately on that device.
